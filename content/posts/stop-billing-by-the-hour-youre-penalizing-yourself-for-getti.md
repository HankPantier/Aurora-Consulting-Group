Most real estate investors we meet in Visalia already sense that their CPA bill has more to do with how the firm operates than how many properties they own. One client who came to us last year owned eight rental units split between an LLC and an S-corp, plus a fourplex he'd picked up through a 1031 exchange. His previous accountant billed hourly. The year he finally moved his bookkeeping onto QuickBooks Online and automated his bank feeds, his invoice went up, not down. Preparation actually took less staff time. The bill just found new line items to fill the hours saved.

That's not one bad firm having an off year. It's the built-in flaw of hourly billing, and it's worth understanding whether you're sizing up your current CPA relationship or just wondering why your fee keeps climbing even as your own records get cleaner.

## Hourly billing quietly punishes the firm for getting faster

Here's the mechanic nobody explains upfront: when a firm bills by the hour, every efficiency it gains, better software, a cleaner chart of accounts, automated depreciation schedules, shows up as lost revenue on the firm's side. A CPA firm that gets faster at reconciling your rental income or tracking your cost segregation study is, financially speaking, cutting its own paycheck. So the incentive runs backward. The firm has no financial reason to adopt tools that save time, and every reason to let a return take as long as it takes.

For a real estate investor, this shows up in specific ways. Depreciation schedules across multiple properties are tedious to track by hand, and tedious work fills hours. Untangling which expenses belong to which LLC when you're running three or four entities takes time, and time is exactly what an hourly firm gets paid for. None of this means your CPA is padding the bill on purpose. It means the pricing model rewards slowness whether anyone intends it to or not.

## Fixed monthly pricing changes who benefits from automation

Aurora prices differently. Every client gets a fixed monthly fee, no annual contract, set around the actual complexity of their business, not a running clock. That single change flips the incentive. When we build out a client's technology stack, whether that's a direct QuickBooks Online feed from a property management platform or automated 1099 tracking through Gusto for subcontractors, the time we save shows up as better margin for us and more advisory time for you, not a smaller bill that discourages us from improving anything.

You get the depth of three specialists for less than the cost of one full-time hire, and none of us are billing you by the tick of a clock for using the tools that make your data cleaner.

## What this looks like for a multi-property, multi-entity portfolio

Say you're holding rental property in Tulare County under an LLC, running a short-term rental in Kings County under a second entity, and considering a cost segregation study on a commercial property you closed in Fresno County. Under hourly billing, every one of those moving pieces is a separate meter running. Under a fixed monthly fee, they're part of the relationship you're already paying for.

| | Hourly billing | Fixed monthly pricing |
|---|---|---|
| Add a new rental entity | New setup hours billed | Included in your existing plan, adjusted at renewal if scope truly changes |
| Firm adopts faster bookkeeping software | Bill often stays the same or rises | Time saved goes toward advisory conversations |
| Quarterly estimated tax check-in before a purchase | Billed as a separate consultation | Built into ongoing tax strategy |
| Cost segregation study coordination | Hours tracked and invoiced separately | Part of proactive planning, no surprise invoice |

The difference isn't just cost predictability, though that matters when your own cash flow already swings with rent collection cycles and closing dates. It's that a fixed-fee firm has a reason to call you before a decision, not just to log a return after one.

## The profession is already moving this direction

This isn't a fringe idea. The [AICPA's Firm Practice Management resources](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/firm-practice-management) track the same shift happening across the profession, away from billable hours and toward fixed and subscription pricing that rewards efficiency instead of penalizing it. Firms that automate well and still bill by the hour are working against their own progress. Firms built around value pricing get to keep what automation creates, and pass some of it forward to clients in the form of more advisory time.

## Common Questions

**What is value-based pricing for an accounting firm?**
Value-based pricing sets your fee around the complexity and outcome of the work, such as the number of entities you run or the tax strategy involved, rather than the hours a staff member logs. Aurora structures this as a fixed monthly fee so you know the cost before the year starts, not after the invoice arrives.

**Does fixed-fee pricing mean unlimited advice, or is there a catch?**
It means the advisory conversations that matter, quarterly tax check-ins, entity structure questions, cash flow planning around a purchase, are built into the relationship instead of billed as add-ons. Scope still matters; a client adding a fourth entity or moving into a new state will see that reflected at renewal, discussed upfront, never as a surprise line item.

**How does fixed pricing work for an investor with multiple properties or entities?**
Your fee is set around your actual complexity: how many entities you run, how many properties need depreciation tracking, whether you're dealing with a cost segregation study or multi-state filings. It's reviewed at renewal, not adjusted mid-year because your accountant had a busy week.

**Is subscription pricing the same as value-based pricing?**
They're close cousins. Subscription pricing is usually a flat recurring fee for a defined scope of ongoing work, while value-based pricing can flex around a specific project or outcome. Aurora's model blends both: a fixed monthly fee for your ongoing accounting and tax work, sized to your actual complexity rather than a clock.
