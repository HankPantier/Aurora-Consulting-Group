## Financial guidance built for contractors and construction businesses

Construction margins run thin, and one underbid job or one late payment from a general contractor can turn a profitable quarter into a scramble. Contractors need more than a CPA who shows up in March. Aurora Consulting Group works alongside contractors and construction business owners across Visalia, Tulare County, Kings County, Fresno County, and Kern County, tracking job costs, payroll, and tax obligations throughout the year, not just at filing time.

Every contractor we work with juggles multiple active jobs, subcontractor crews, and material costs that shift week to week. Our Team of 3, a Client Service Manager, Client Controller, and Client CFO, gives you the visibility of three financial professionals for less than the cost of one full-time hire. You get answers before a cost overrun becomes a loss, not after it shows up on a tax return.

## Job costing and project profitability tracking

Knowing whether a job made money shouldn't require waiting for a year-end report. Your Client Controller sets up job costing systems that track labor, materials, subcontractor costs, and overhead against each project's budget, so you can see which jobs are actually profitable and which ones are quietly draining cash.

Frequent advisory check-ins, not a single annual meeting, are what catch cost overruns while there's still time to act. If a project's material costs run 15% over budget in week three, you'll know in week three, not in April. Your Client CFO reviews profitability across active jobs on a schedule that matches your job cycles, flagging trends before they turn into a surprise on next year's return.

That level of detail also makes bidding future work easier. When you know your true cost per square foot or per crew-hour on past projects, your next bid is built on real numbers instead of a guess.

## Payroll, subcontractor compliance, and 1099 management

Running payroll for a crew and issuing 1099s to subcontractors is one of the highest-risk parts of a construction business. Misclassify a worker, miss a filing deadline, or lose track of a sub's W-9, and the penalties land on you, not on the sub.

We handle:
- Weekly or biweekly payroll processing for your crew, including workers' comp reporting
- Subcontractor W-9 collection and 1099-NEC issuance at year-end
- Worker classification review, so employees and subcontractors are categorized correctly from the start
- Multi-jurisdiction payroll tax filings when your crews cross county or state lines

As a Gusto Partner, we build payroll workflows on a platform that ties directly into your books, so labor costs land in job costing reports without double entry.

## Tax strategy and quarterly planning for construction businesses

Construction income doesn't arrive in even amounts. A big draw in June and a slow month in November can throw off quarterly estimated payments if nobody's watching your numbers between tax seasons. Your Client CFO builds a quarterly tax plan around your actual cash flow, adjusting estimates as jobs close and new contracts start, so April doesn't bring a bill you didn't see coming.

Entity structure matters just as much. Whether you're operating as a sole proprietor, LLC, or S-corp affects self-employment tax, how you pay yourself, and how equipment purchases and depreciation flow through your return. We walk through those decisions as your revenue grows, not as a one-time setup conversation you never revisit.

If the IRS ever sends a letter, our Enrolled Agents carry federal representation authority and can speak with the IRS directly on your behalf. You won't be the one on the phone trying to decode a notice.
