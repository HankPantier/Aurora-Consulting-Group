Commission checks don't arrive on a schedule, and neither should your financial support. Real estate brokers in Visalia, Tulare County, and across Central California deal with a rhythm most CPAs never see up close: months of pipeline building followed by a closing that finally clears escrow. A once-a-year tax preparer misses that rhythm entirely.

Aurora Consulting Group assigns every broker client a Team of 3, a Client Service Manager, Client Controller, and Client CFO, working together for less than the cost of one full-time hire. That means someone is watching your numbers between closings, not just in March. We build tax strategy and bookkeeping around how you actually get paid, with fixed monthly pricing and no annual contract locking you in.

If you're growing a book of business, building a team, or weighing a move toward brokerage ownership, this is the kind of embedded advisory relationship that pays for itself.

## Smoothing out commission-cycle cash flow

A slow month after a big closing can feel like a crisis even when your annual production is strong. That gap between deals is the single biggest financial stress point we hear from brokers, and it's solvable with the right structure.

Our Client Controllers build reserve targets based on your actual closing history, not generic percentages. We help you set aside a portion of each commission before it hits your personal account, so a slow quarter doesn't force decisions you'll regret. For brokers running a small team or splitting commissions with agents, we also track payables and draws so cash doesn't quietly evaporate between deals.

This isn't a one-time budget exercise. It's a recurring conversation, built into your monthly check-ins, that adjusts as your production changes.

## Self-employment tax and quarterly estimated payments made simple

Self-employment tax catches independent brokers off guard almost every year, usually around the same time an underpayment penalty notice shows up. Our Enrolled Agents, credentialed with IRS representation authority, calculate your quarterly estimates using real-time production data instead of last year's guesswork.

What that looks like in practice:

- Quarterly estimate recalculations as your commission pipeline shifts
- Self-employment tax planning that accounts for retirement contributions and deductions
- Direct IRS representation if a notice or audit question comes up
- Coordination with your entity structure to reduce what's owed at the self-employment tax rate
- A clear calendar so payment dates never come as a surprise

Because our Enrolled Agents can represent you directly before the IRS, you're never navigating a notice alone or paying a separate firm to interpret it.

## Tracking expenses, mileage, and marketing spend with clarity

Mileage logs, MLS fees, staging costs, open house signage, client gifts: brokers accumulate dozens of small deductible expenses that add up to real tax savings, if they're actually captured. Most brokers lose track of half of them by Q3.

Our outsourced accounting team builds a bookkeeping system, connected to your bank feeds and mileage tracking app, that categorizes these expenses as they happen instead of during a frantic January reconstruction. We flag marketing spend by campaign so you can see which listings, sponsorships, or lead sources are actually generating return, not just which ones felt productive.

By the time your Client CFO sits down for your quarterly review, the numbers are already clean. That means the conversation is about strategy, not data entry.

## Choosing the right entity structure as your production grows

Many brokers start as sole proprietors and never revisit that decision, even after production doubles. Once self-employment tax on net income starts outweighing the cost of running payroll, an S-corp election usually makes sense, but the timing and the numbers behind it matter more than the general rule.

Our Client CFOs model the actual break-even point for your production level before recommending a change, and coordinate the election, payroll setup, and reasonable-compensation analysis so it's done correctly the first time. For brokers expanding into a brokerage, property management arm, or a second entity, we bring the same multi-entity and multi-state expertise we apply to [multi-state and multi-entity businesses](/industries/multi-state-multi-entity-businesses), including intercompany transactions and consolidated reporting.

Getting the structure right early avoids a messy, expensive correction later.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Real Estate Brokers

**Q: How do you handle commission-based income for tax planning?**
A: We build reserve targets and quarterly estimate calculations from your actual closing history, not generic percentages. This smooths the gap between commission checks and keeps your quarterly tax payments accurate as production changes throughout the year.

**Q: Should real estate brokers form an S-corp?**
A: It depends on your production level and self-employment tax exposure. Our Client CFOs model your specific break-even point before recommending an S-corp election, then handle the election, payroll setup, and reasonable-compensation analysis.

**Q: Can you represent me if the IRS sends a notice?**
A: Yes. Our Enrolled Agents hold IRS representation authority, meaning they can communicate and negotiate with the IRS directly on your behalf, without routing you to a separate tax attorney or third party.

**Q: What deductions do real estate brokers often miss?**
A: Mileage, MLS fees, staging costs, marketing spend by campaign, and client gifts are the most commonly underreported deductions. Our bookkeeping system categorizes these as they happen so nothing gets lost before tax season.

**Q: Do you support brokers who are hiring agents or assistants?**
A: Yes. As a Gusto Partner, we set up and manage payroll, handle 1099 versus W-2 classification, and track corporate compliance obligations as your team grows.
