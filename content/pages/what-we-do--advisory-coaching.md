## Coaching that's part of your Team of 3, not an add-on

Advisory and coaching at Aurora Consulting Group isn't a line item tacked onto your monthly invoice. It's built into the Team of 3 model you already have: your Client Service Manager, Client Controller, and Client CFO. Once your books are accurate and your taxes are planned, a different question shows up. What do you do with this information? That's the gap coaching fills.

We run two coaching tracks side by side, one focused on the financial mechanics of growing a business, the other on the person running it. Entrepreneurs across Visalia, Tulare County, and Central California come back to this pairing because spreadsheets alone don't build a business, and neither does motivation alone. You need both, delivered by people who already know your numbers.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-right | image: coaching-strategy-session.jpg | alt: "Client CFO and business owner reviewing financial strategy on a laptop" | query: "business coaching session laptop meeting" -->
## Two tracks of support, built around how your business grows

The financial strategy track is led by your Client CFO and centers on the decisions that move revenue: cash flow forecasting, pricing, KPI tracking, and the timing question every growing business faces, which is when to hire, when to invest, and when to wait. This isn't a quarterly review buried in a slide deck. It's a working relationship where your CFO already knows your numbers and can talk through a decision the week it comes up, not the month after.

The second track is personal and leadership coaching, built for the entrepreneur, not just the entity. Delegation, mindset under pressure, work-life balance, and career pathing for a growing team all belong here. A contractor scaling from one crew to three faces different leadership questions than a solo consultant hiring a first employee, and this track flexes to fit either. Most clients use both tracks. Some lean harder on one for a season and shift as priorities change.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: with-image | image: hr-compliance-coaching.jpg | alt: "Advisor discussing HR compliance documents with a business owner" | query: "advisor discussing documents office" -->
## Guidance for the decisions accounting alone can't answer

A clean set of books tells you what happened. It doesn't tell you whether to classify your next hire as a W-2 employee or a 1099 contractor, or whether your business insurance actually covers the risk you're carrying now that you have five employees instead of two. These are the questions that land on an entrepreneur's desk with no obvious place to send them, and they're exactly where our coaching sessions earn their keep.

- HR decisions: hiring timelines, contractor vs. employee classification, and compensation structure as headcount grows
- Corporate compliance: entity maintenance, registered agent requirements, and multi-state registration obligations
- Insurance gaps: coverage reviews tied to revenue growth, new locations, or added risk exposure
- Workforce planning: career pathing and org structure as a five-person team becomes a fifteen-person team

icon: Briefcase

Legal, medical, financial, engineering, and real estate professionals need coaching that respects billable-hour economics. We help you separate overhead from client-facing costs and plan around income that swings with caseload or project volume.

### Technology Companies
icon: Lightbulb

From early-stage SaaS startups to established e-commerce operations, tech clients need coaching that keeps pace with fundraising rounds, burn rate, and recurring revenue metrics that traditional accounting reports don't surface on their own.

### Nonprofit Organizations

### Multi-State and Multi-Entity Businesses
icon: Building2

## What working with your Client CFO looks like

### Onboarding and strategy mapping
We start by mapping your current entity structure, cash flow patterns, and the decisions you're facing over the next twelve months. Your Client CFO builds the coaching agenda around what's actually on your plate.

### Monthly advisory meetings
Most clients meet monthly, not annually. That cadence is what catches a pricing problem or a compliance deadline while there's still time to act on it.

### Quarterly deep dives
Four times a year, we step back from the month-to-month and look at KPIs, tax positioning, and whether your entity structure still fits your growth stage.

### Direct access between meetings
Questions don't wait for a scheduled call. You reach your Team of 3 directly, and pricing stays fixed each month with no annual contract locking you in.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Business Coaching Services | Aurora Consulting Group LLC

**Q: What is included in business coaching services at Aurora Consulting Group?**
A: Coaching includes two tracks: financial strategy coaching covering cash flow, KPIs, and pricing decisions, and personal leadership coaching covering mindset, delegation, and work-life balance. Both are delivered by your Client CFO as part of the Team of 3 model.

**Q: How often will I meet with my Client CFO?**
A: Most clients meet monthly, with quarterly deep dives to review KPIs, tax positioning, and entity structure. Direct access between meetings is included, so questions don't wait for a scheduled call.

**Q: Is coaching priced separately from accounting and tax services?**
A: Coaching is billed under the same fixed monthly pricing structure as your other services, with no annual contract. Your rate reflects the full Team of 3 engagement, not a per-session coaching fee.

**Q: Does coaching help with HR and compliance questions, not just numbers?**
A: Yes. Sessions regularly cover hiring classification, corporate compliance, insurance coverage gaps, and workforce planning, the decisions that accounting reports alone don't answer.

**Q: Which types of businesses benefit most from this coaching model?**
A: Professionals and consultants, technology companies, nonprofits, and multi-state or multi-entity businesses see the most value, given the frequent advisory touchpoints and entity-specific strategy the model provides.
