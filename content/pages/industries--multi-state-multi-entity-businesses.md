## Growing across state lines creates accounting complexity we solve daily

A second location in Nevada. A holding company in Texas. A remote hire who quietly triggers nexus in a state you've never filed in. Growth like this is good news until the compliance mail starts arriving from agencies you didn't know you owed anything to.

Business owners across Tulare County, Kings County, Fresno County, Kern County, and the rest of Central California come to us the moment expansion outpaces their bookkeeping. Registration deadlines get missed. Intercompany loans go untracked. Nobody's quite sure which LLC owns which asset anymore.

We built this practice around exactly that problem: entrepreneurs running multiple entities across multiple states who need someone already fluent in the mess. You don't need a lecture on nexus rules. You need a team that's already registered a business in a new state this quarter and knows which form gets filed first.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-right | image: team-of-three-advisory-meeting.jpg | alt: "Three accounting professionals reviewing financial reports together at a table" | query: "team meeting financial advisors office" -->
## Your team of three keeps every entity and every state in sync

Every Aurora client gets a [Team of Three](/meet-the-team/your-team): a Client Service Manager who keeps communication tight, a Client Controller who owns the books for each entity, and a Client CFO who reads the consolidated picture and translates it into decisions. For less than the cost of one full-time controller, you get three specialists who talk to each other daily instead of one generalist stretched across everything.

That structure matters once you're running three LLCs and an S-corp holding company. Someone needs to reconcile intercompany loans between the operating entity in Fresno and the property entity in Arizona. Someone needs to know that California's franchise tax rules don't match Nevada's. Someone needs to turn all of that into a package your bank or your board can actually read.

Your Client Controller handles entity-level bookkeeping day to day. Your Client CFO builds the consolidated view and flags what's shifting quarter over quarter. Between them, your Client Service Manager makes sure nothing falls through the cracks. Building an internal finance department first isn't a prerequisite.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: with-image-left | image: multi-entity-financial-reports.jpg | alt: "Accountant reviewing consolidated financial statements for multiple business entities" | query: "accountant reviewing financial statements office" -->
## Multi-entity consolidation and reporting built to stay clean

Running LLCs and an S-corp holding structure across state lines means intercompany transactions pile up fast: management fees between entities, shared payroll allocations, loans that need documented terms. Get the treatment wrong and you're looking at reclassified income or a blown pass-through election.

We build consolidation schedules that show what your business is doing across every entity, alongside the standalone detail your CPA needs come tax season. In practice, that means:

- Consolidated financial statements alongside standalone entity-level reporting
- Intercompany transaction tracking with proper elimination entries
- Ownership structure documentation that stays current as entities change
- A multi-entity chart of accounts built for clean cross-entity comparison
- Reporting packages formatted for lenders, investors, or your board

## Multi-state tax planning and compliance handled proactively, not reactively

Nexus doesn't wait for you to notice it. Hire one remote employee in Colorado, cross an economic nexus threshold in Texas, or open a warehouse in Arizona, and you've created new filing obligations whether or not anyone told you. We track where those thresholds sit for every state you touch and register your entities before a state notices for you.

That includes quarterly estimated payments calculated correctly across jurisdictions, state-specific apportionment strategy, and franchise tax deadlines that rarely align with your federal calendar. Our [tax planning services](/services/tax-planning-strategy) are led by Enrolled Agents carrying IRS representation authority, which matters when a notice arrives referencing activity in a state you thought was settled two years ago.

Multi-state tax planning isn't a once-a-year conversation for our clients. It's part of the same advisory meetings where we review your books, so a new hire in another state gets flagged before it becomes a compliance problem instead of after.

## HR, payroll, and compliance support for every state you operate in

Payroll taxes work differently once you cross a state line. Withholding rules, unemployment insurance rates, paid leave mandates, and worker classification standards all shift by jurisdiction, and getting one wrong stacks penalties on top of the tax you already owe. We handle this inside your regular accounting cycle, not as a separate line item billed by the hour.

### Multi-state payroll compliance
icon: Users

Withholding registrations, unemployment insurance accounts, and paid leave requirements tracked for every state where you have an employee, contractor, or remote worker.

### Corporate and HR compliance
icon: ClipboardCheck

Annual report filings, registered agent updates, and HR policy gaps flagged before they become fines, especially for entities operating under different names across state lines.

### Insurance and risk considerations
icon: ShieldCheck

Coordination on workers' comp, general liability, and coverage gaps that surface when a business adds employees or property in a new jurisdiction.

There's no annual contract locking you in, either. If your structure changes, your plan changes with it, on 30 days' notice instead of a signed agreement you're stuck honoring.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Multi-State / Multi-Entity Businesses

A: We track economic and physical presence thresholds for payroll, sales, and property in every state you operate, then register entities and adjust filings before a state sends a notice, not after.

**Q: Can you handle consolidated reporting across multiple LLCs and an S-corp?**
A: Yes. Your Client Controller maintains entity-level books while your Client CFO builds consolidated financials with proper intercompany eliminations, giving you the detail and the full picture in one reporting package.

**Q: What happens if my business gets a tax notice from a state we rarely think about?**
A: Our Enrolled Agents hold IRS representation authority and handle state correspondence directly, so you're not the one explaining transactions from eighteen months ago on a call with an agency.

**Q: Does pricing change every time we add a new entity or state?**
A: Your fixed monthly fee is set for your current entity count and state footprint, and we adjust it as your structure changes, not per hour or per filing, with no annual contract required.
