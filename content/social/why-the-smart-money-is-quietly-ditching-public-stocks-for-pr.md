## LinkedIn

A physician client in Visalia almost moved bond-ladder money into a private credit fund yielding two points higher, without knowing the K-1 that fund sends in March looks nothing like the 1099-DIV she was used to. That timing gap causes real headaches: unplanned extensions, or amended returns once the K-1 numbers land. It gets more complicated when the fund holds assets across state lines. We've seen California investors get a K-1 covering Texas, Colorado, and New York holdings and suddenly owe three state filings for one investment. There's also UBTI risk for anyone holding these funds inside a self-directed IRA. None of this means avoiding private markets. It means planning before you wire the money, not after the K-1 shows up. Our Team of 3, a Client Service Manager, Client Controller, and Client CFO, models the after-tax return before you commit capital.
