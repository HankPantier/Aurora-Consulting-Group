## LinkedIn

A Visalia business owner sold a rental property, moved the proceeds into a brokerage account, and picked a mix of funds. Eighteen months later, one stock had doubled and quietly grown to 40% of the account, with nobody deciding to take on that risk.

That's the problem with a 60/40 portfolio: a 20% run in equities while bonds stay flat can push it to 68/32 without a single trade. For professionals, consultants, and multi-entity business owners, picking better funds once won't fix that drift. Rebalancing on a schedule or by cash flow does, especially when it happens inside tax-advantaged accounts first, before triggering capital gains in a taxable one.
